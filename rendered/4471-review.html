Parser: shared parser reads month first

Day-first tenant dates are read month first by the shared parser. For example, R-41200 reports a 7 April reminder becoming 4 July. App and email reports, and form reports whose hint agrees with the tenant’s date order, belong to this group under the current logic. Fix the parser to respect the tenant’s locale and test ambiguous dates with both parts at most 12, plus unambiguous dates. Keep this separate from the form-hint fix.

Hint: booking-v3 tells day-first users to enter month first

The booking-v3 form displays MM/DD/YYYY while the form reads the date in the tenant’s locale order. This affects form reports from Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour. For example, R-41230 describes 6 January becoming 1 June. These reports were merged into 4471 but have no separate ticket in the export. Match the hint to the actual input parser and locale, and test the displayed hint and resulting date together.

Five reports need a separate investigation

The table’s footnote is a warning, not proof that a date swap explains every body. R-41202 says 12 May became 11 May; R-41209 says June bookings are one day off; R-41236 says the invite arrived before the booking; R-41344 reports different weekdays in Lisbon and Toronto; R-41620 says 6 October showed as Monday. They remain included in the parser group according to the supplied classifier; do not double-count them or silently claim a confirmed timezone bug. Inspect original timestamps, timezone conversion and booking/invite logs before deciding whether to split another cause. The export alone cannot establish that root cause.

Reports arrived before the merge spike

The two series measure different events: receivedAt is when a report arrived; mergedAt is when the desk filed it into 4471. Reports arrive from April onward. The large July merge spike is a backlog being filed, not evidence that the bug began in July or that reports suddenly rose then. Continued arrivals after July show the issue remained active in this export; a merge is not a fix.
